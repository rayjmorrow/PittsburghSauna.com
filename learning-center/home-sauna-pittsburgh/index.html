How to choose a home sauna in Pittsburgh.

Start with the experience you want, then match the sauna to your space, electrical setup and budget. That order usually produces a better decision than choosing a model first.

Four decisions narrow the field quickly.

Heat

Traditional or infrared?

Traditional gives you hotter room air, stones and optional steam. Infrared uses radiant heat at lower air temperatures and is often easier to work into an everyday routine.

Compare Them
Location

Indoor or outdoor?

Indoor saunas can take advantage of basements and finished rooms. Outdoor saunas preserve interior space but require more planning around base, drainage and access.

Compare Locations
Size

How many people?

Buy for normal use, not the biggest gathering you can imagine. A properly sized sauna is easier to place, heat and enjoy regularly.

Budget

What should it cost?

Current complete infrared examples start in the mid-$4,000s, while traditional projects add heater and installation variables.
