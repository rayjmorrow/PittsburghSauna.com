What a traditional sauna feels like

Traditional saunas heat the air and the sauna room around you. Typical home-sauna operating temperatures are much higher than infrared, and the heated stones let you create short bursts of steam by adding water. For many buyers, that ritual is the reason to choose traditional over infrared.

Plan the heater and electrical work before installation

Traditional electric saunas normally require a dedicated hardwired circuit sized to the heater. The heater is matched to the sauna volume rather than chosen by price alone. Our traditional sauna models pair with compatible Harvia heater packages, and we recommend confirming the exact heater before an electrician begins work.

See our sauna electrical requirements guide and Pittsburgh sauna installation-cost guide for planning details.

Indoor or outdoor?

Traditional heat works well in both settings. Indoor rooms are convenient for basements, home gyms and dedicated wellness rooms. Outdoor cabins, cubes and barrels create a more destination-style backyard experience. If the sauna will be outside, base preparation, drainage, access and electrical routing become part of the project.
