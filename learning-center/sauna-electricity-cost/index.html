How Much Electricity Does a Sauna Use?

The answer is mostly math, not mystery. Electricity use depends on the heater’s kilowatt rating, how long it runs, how often you use the sauna and your local price per kilowatt-hour.

The basic math

Start with kilowatts × time.

A 2.4 kW heater running for one hour can use up to 2.4 kWh of electricity. A 9 kW heater running for one hour can use up to 9 kWh. Real operation can vary as controls cycle heating elements to maintain temperature.

Estimated energy cost = kW × hours of operation × your electric rate per kWh

Use this as a planning estimate, then plug in the actual heater rating and the electricity rate shown on your utility bill.

What changes the bill?

Five variables matter most.

Heater output

A larger kW heater can draw more power while actively heating.

Warm-up time

Traditional saunas usually need more preheat time than infrared.

Session length

Longer total run time means more energy use.

Frequency

Three sessions a week and seven sessions a week produce very different monthly totals.

Cabin & conditions

Sauna size, construction, glass, ambient temperature and outdoor weather can affect how hard the heater works.

Your electric rate

The same sauna can cost different amounts to operate in different utility territories.

Infrared vs. traditional

Infrared generally starts with a smaller heater load.

For perspective, MAAX Saunas lists its Infrared 160 at 2.4 kW. Its buying guidance describes traditional electric heater packages in the roughly 6–9 kW range. That does not mean traditional is “wasteful”—it is producing a very different high-temperature sauna experience.

We intentionally do not publish a fake universal “cost per month.” Your model, habits and utility rate determine that number. Once pricing is loaded into Pittsburgh Sauna, we can also show realistic operating examples alongside specific models.
Comparing ownership costs?

Start with the sauna experience you want, then compare the numbers.

A lower electric bill is not much of a bargain if you bought the wrong kind of sauna. We recommend choosing traditional vs. infrared first, then comparing size, heater output and operating cost among the models that actually fit you.
