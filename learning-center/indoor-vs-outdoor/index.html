Indoor vs. Outdoor Sauna: Where Should Yours Go?

Both can give you a great sauna experience. The real difference is what you want the sauna to become: an easy part of your everyday routine inside the house, or a destination that becomes part of your backyard.

Choose indoor if…

Convenience matters most, you have suitable interior space, and you want the shortest walk possible between everyday life and your sauna.

Choose outdoor if…

You want a backyard retreat, prefer to preserve indoor square footage, or want the sauna itself to become part of your outdoor living space.

Side by side

The experience starts before you turn on the heat.

Location changes more than appearance. It affects installation, access, electrical planning, weather exposure and how naturally the sauna fits into your routine.

Indoor Sauna

Convenience wins.

Best locationsBasements, spare rooms, large bathrooms, garages and other suitable interior spaces.
Site prepA level, appropriate floor, enough ceiling height and the required electrical supply for the exact model.
WeatherNo rain, snow or exterior weather exposure to plan around.
Daily useEasy access can make a short sauna session feel like a normal part of the day rather than an event.
Trade-offThe sauna uses interior floor space and still needs to fit the room, electrical plan and manufacturer clearances.
Outdoor Sauna

Create a destination.

Best locationsPatios, backyard wellness areas and other sites with appropriate access, drainage and a prepared base.
Site prepA flat, level, solid and permanent foundation appropriate for the specific sauna and site.
WeatherThe sauna must be specifically designed for outdoor use and installed according to its weather and site requirements.
Daily useThe walk outside, especially in cool weather, can become part of the ritual and make the sauna feel like an escape.
Trade-offMore planning up front: base, drainage, delivery access, electrical routing and possibly local permit or zoning questions.
Important: an indoor sauna should not simply be moved outside. Outdoor models are designed for exterior exposure. Always follow the exact manufacturer requirements for the model and installation location you choose.
Which sounds more like your home?

Think about how you’ll actually use it.

You may prefer indoor if…

  • You want to use the sauna frequently with almost no transition time.
  • You already have a basement, spare room, bathroom or garage area that makes sense.
  • You want infrared and like the idea of a compact wellness space.
  • You would rather avoid outdoor site preparation.
  • Privacy and easy year-round access matter more than creating a backyard feature.

Outdoor planning

Four things to solve before delivery day.

Outdoor sauna projects are straightforward when the site is planned first. These are the questions worth answering before choosing the final model.

1. The base

The sauna needs a stable, level surface that can properly support the structure. Hekla’s outdoor guidance calls for a flat, level, solid and permanent base; a concrete slab or reinforced paving over a solid sub-base are examples.

2. Drainage

Rain and snowmelt should move away from the sauna rather than collect around or beneath it. The prettiest location in the yard is not necessarily the best location if it stays wet.

3. Access

Think about how the sauna and its components will reach the site. Gates, fences, landscaping, rooflines and narrow side yards can matter just as much as the final footprint.

4. Electricity

Plan power after you choose the actual sauna and heater. Voltage, circuit size and installation requirements vary by model, and outdoor electrical work should be planned for the specific site.

One more question: permits

Local requirements can vary by municipality and project. Before site work begins, confirm whether your location, electrical work or structure triggers local permit, setback or zoning requirements.

Don’t guess first.

Choose the sauna, verify its footprint and installation requirements, then build the site around the real specifications. That order prevents expensive rework.
